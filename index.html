The Geometric Signature of Decision-Making in Large Language Models

TL;DR. Across five LLMs, isotropy rises through the early layers, peaks, then drops sharply at a critical layer. Task-relevant clusters appear at that exact layer, and the steepness of the drop predicts accuracy. Preventing the cliff by intervening at layer L+1 wipes out the clusters and cuts accuracy by up to 38 points.

Key takeaways

01A cliff, not a slopeIsoScore climbs to a peak at layer L, then falls sharply at L+1. ID instead shows a gradual hump.
02Clusters form right thereThe k-NN score jumps and CKA dips at the same layers: a one-step reorganization of the representation space.
03Steepness predicts accuracyBigger drops go with higher accuracy, across prompt formats and 0/2/4-shot, and beat intrinsic dimension as a predictor.
04It is causalRemoving the top principal components at layer L+1 prevents the cliff, destroys the clusters, and cuts accuracy by up to 38 points.

What is isotropy?

Isotropy measures how evenly variance is spread over directions. IsoScore is 1 for a perfectly round cloud and near 0 when variance collapses onto a few directions.

Drag to concentrate the variance. A drop in isotropy at the cliff means the model sacrifices uniformity to compress information into a task-relevant, lower-dimensional manifold.

Why it matters

  
The isotropy cliff gives a simple geometric handle on where LLMs commit to an answer.

  
    
    
  • A tool for locating critical layers. The isotropy drop, the k-NN jump and the CKA dip coincide, which points to the layers where task-relevant structure forms.
    • 
    
  • A sharper lens than intrinsic dimension. ID rises and then compresses gradually (a "hunchback"). Isotropy instead drops abruptly, lines up better with the emergence of clusters, and its steepness predicts accuracy better.
    • 
    
  • The answer becomes readable at the cliff. Decoding the answer directly from the hidden state is near chance just before it and jumps right after (Qwen3-8B: 26.8% → 82.0%, Llama-3.1-8B: 29.3% → 65.3%).
    • 
    
  • Not a prompt artifact. The cliff shows up across four prompt formats and 0/2/4-shot in-context learning, so it looks like a general feature of how these models process the question.
    • 
  

  
Limitations
    
    
      
  • Finding the transition layers. We identify them by inspecting the layer-wise profiles and the CKA change. This is not yet a fully deterministic criterion.
    • 
      
  • Adjacent layers only. Some models (Qwen2.5-3B, Mistral-7B-v0.3) show several shifts, so broader layer spans may reveal multi-stage restructuring.
    • 
      
  • Scope. Five model families and multiple-choice QA. Open-ended generation is the next step.
    • 
      
  • Qwen3 recovery. Qwen3-8B rebuilds its clusters after the intervention (see the last chapter). Why some architectures compensate and others do not is still open.
    • 
    

  






  
Is the cliff causal?

  
Yes, at layer L+1. We remove the top-10 principal components there (all-but-the-top, about 0.05–1% of the dimensions), which raises isotropy and prevents the sudden drop. Components are estimated on 4,000 OpenBookQA samples; accuracy is measured on 1,000 unseen ones.

    Limitation: Qwen3-8B loses its clusters, then regains them
    

      
      
    

    
    
Approximate redraw of Figure 8 (grey band = transition, layers 23→24).

    
Qwen3-8B is the exception. The intervention erases its clusters at the cliff (ARI ≈ 0.00), but the model rebuilds them: ARI and early-decoding accuracy re-emerge around layer 28, about five layers later than in the original, and early decoding returns to ≈0.8. Final accuracy drops only 3.1 points (85.6% → 82.4%). The cliff is the most impactful decision site, but not necessarily the only one, and why some architectures can compensate remains open.
